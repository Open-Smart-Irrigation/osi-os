# PocketMaestro: catalogue plan (draft 0.2)

Companion to [spec.md](spec.md) section 9, regraded after the panel review
(panel items 29-36). Everything here is the organist's to revise; the
grading scale and the grade-1 rung are the parts most worth arguing about.

## 1. Grading scale

Six grades, defined by the hardest sustained demand. Pedal technique is two
axes, not one progression: the *alternate-toe* (early) axis carries the
Baroque repertoire, the *heel-toe* (modern) axis the Romantic; a piece is
graded on the axis its style calls for, and pieces where both are
defensible ship both editorial layers.

| Grade | Pedal (either axis) | Manuals | Texture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slow bass line, stepwise, no crossing; or pedal ad libitum | Chordal or two-voice, one manual | Chorale harmonisation or quiet character piece |
| 2 | Walking line, small leaps; first crossings (early axis) or first heel use (modern axis) | Ornamented line over accompaniment; a second manual in play | Trio-lite textures at moderate tempo |
| 3 | Independent pedal voice; crossings and alternation at speed, or simple heel-toe | Cantus in an inner voice, two manuals throughout | Ritornello, variation sets, sustained independence |
| 4 | Pedal melody or continuous motion; wide leaps; the axis's full toolkit | Rapid figuration, frequent manual changes | Stamina at a fast steady tempo |
| 5 | Pedal solo passages, double pedal | Four-voice fugue, virtuoso passagework | Free sections, long spans |
| 6 | Pedal theme against fast figuration throughout | Toccata figuration at high tempo | Sustained virtuosity |

The ladder is climbable by a pianist with no pedal experience starting at
grade 1; the free lesson's pedal-orientation on-ramp comes before its first
pedal exercise.

## 2. Sources and licensing

Public domain in Switzerland and the EU: composer dead more than 70 years.
The engraving source follows the declared hierarchy — autograph or
composer-supervised print first, 19th-century collected editions only where
nothing earlier survives — and the exact source, date, and plate are
recorded in each package manifest.

| Composer | Died | Preferred source | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| J. S. Bach | 1750 | Orgelbuechlein autograph (P 283); Schuebler original print with Bach's corrections | Bach-Gesellschaft only as a cross-check; Peters (Griepenkerl) never as text — its slurs and dynamics are a Romantic layer. |
| Buxtehude | 1707 | Earliest manuscript traditions via the old collected editions | Spitta as fallback, flagged editorial. |
| Pachelbel | 1706 | Denkmaeler der Tonkunst in Bayern (1901) | |
| Brahms | 1897 | Simrock first edition of op. 122 (1902) | |
| Mendelssohn | 1847 | Coventry first edition of op. 65 (1845) | |
| Franck | 1890 | Enoch first edition of L'Organiste (1892); Durand for the larger works | |
| Boellmann | 1897 | Durand first edition (1895) | |
| Widor | 1937 | A named Hamelle issue of Symphony no. 5, chosen and documented — the Toccata's readings and tempo differ between issues | |

Excluded as sources: Dupre's editions (d. 1971), Bornemann, the Neue
Bach-Ausgabe, all modern urtexts. Editorial pedaling in old editions is
copyright-free but not ideology-free; ours is authored fresh on both axes.

Engraving is bought or taken from vetted CC0 community typesettings where
they exist (checked per piece, licence recorded), else engraved in house.

## 4. First quarter after launch

| Grade | Piece | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Pachelbel, Ciacona in F minor | Regraded from 2: the ground is a pedal part and the stamina is real. Pedal required; sectioned by variation groups, which is what makes a chaconne the ideal adaptive piece. |
| 3 | J. S. Bach, "Wachet auf" BWV 645 | Tenor cantus, ritornello, two manuals; Schuebler original print. |
| 3 | Buxtehude, "Nun bitten wir" BuxWV 208 | North German coloratura over pedal; ornamentation-free passages marked. |
| 4 | Boellmann, Toccata from Suite gothique | The heel-toe piece of the set: box technique and the registration build. |
| 4 | Mendelssohn, Sonata no. 6 op. 65, first movement | Titled as the movement it is, with commentary on the Fuga and Finale and why the movement stands alone. |

Then one lesson per month, the sustainable rate. Grade 5-6 full lessons
(BWV 565 complete with its attribution essay and free-section handling, the
Widor) follow when the grade 1-3 shelves are stocked deep enough that no
learner runs out of a next step — depth in the pianist-transition grades is
what retains (panel item 35). Learner request votes reorder this queue from
B3.

## 5. Authoring effort model

Per piece of average length, with the content pipeline in place (MuseScore
source of truth, CLI, annotation tool):

| Step | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Source selection, licence and plate record | 0.5 | |
| Engraving | 1.5 | Bought or CC0 import plus correction; 6+ if engraved from scratch. |
| Proofread on device | 1.5 | Against the source, landscape on the phone. |
| Fingering and both pedaling layers | 4 | Two axes where defensible. |
| Sectioning, annotations, touch categories | 2.5 | Metrical/free flags, voice weights, technique tags. |
| Commentary (DE + EN), analysis and ear items | 5 | Includes source-critical notes. |
| Per-piece achievements | 0.5 | |
| Reference recording, MIDI + audio + film | 3-8 | Scaled by grade; the same session yields the YouTube film. |
| Validation on the console | 2 | Every configuration once, plus a self-assessed walk-through. |
| Total | 21-26 | Grade 5-6 pieces run higher on recording and free-section handling. |
